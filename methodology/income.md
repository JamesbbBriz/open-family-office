# Income durability, not just headline yield

The model separates recurring stable-labelled receipts, recurring variable receipts, one-off receipts,
and returns of principal. These labels describe supplied assumptions; they are not guarantees or calibrated ratings.

A salary can cease. A dividend can be cut. Business revenue may not be distributable cash. Rent must be
distinguished from cash after vacancy, operating costs, financing and tax. A private-fund distribution may
combine investment return with a return of capital. Do not compare these headline amounts as if equivalent.

Only household-attributable net after-tax cash enters this release's budget. The project does not calculate
personal/company tax or translate gross business revenue into disposable household income. Record a known
net amount or a transparently user-specified assumption; gross-only uncertainty blocks the affected budget.

A sale, gift, inheritance or bonus is dated and conditional until confirmed. When selling an existing asset,
remove its equity value and assess which recurring receipts cease. Do not both keep the business's income
forever and spend the sale proceeds. Earn-outs, deferred consideration and taxes require additional explicit
cashflow assumptions; this release only ships a simple single-settlement business-equity sale bridge.

Useful qualitative fields in the accompanying register: evidence quality, counterparty/customer concentration,
expected duration, cyclicality, capital required to maintain distributions and dependency on household labour.
Do not fabricate probabilities, beta coefficients, correlation estimates or an objective 'sustainability score'.
